Your website says "coming soon". Now what?

A short, practical checklist for practices whose new site is still being built — and who still need the phone to ring.

← Back to Zentry

If your site is a holding page, a visitor has usually made up their mind in about five seconds. They either find a way to contact you, or they leave. A "coming soon" banner with nothing underneath reads as "not open yet" — even when you are taking bookings today.

The fix is not a full website. It is one clear page that answers four questions: who you are, where you are, how to reach you right now, and what to do next.

What a holding page must contain

  • Your name and what you do — one line, no jargon. "Family dentistry in Basingstoke" beats "exceptional dentistry, beautifully personal" when someone is looking for a phone number.
  • A tappable phone number — on a phone, a number must be a link that opens the dialler, not text someone has to select.
  • Your address — with the postcode. People check distance before they call.
  • One action — a short enquiry form (name, contact, message) or a Book button. Not both competing for attention.
  • An honest line about the new site — "Our new website is on its way. In the meantime, call or send a short enquiry and we'll get straight back to you."

Three mistakes that cost enquiries

  • Banner plus half-built pages. A "coming soon" banner sitting above live service pages sends two messages at once. Pick one story.
  • Email only. "Please email us" loses the people who want to speak to a human now.
  • No next step on mobile. If the call button is not tappable and the form has five fields, the visitor bounces.
